# One Step Relation Drives Every Mode

The precedents agree on a design, and it is small: define the model language as a nondeterministic small-step relation over configurations in the style of P and Rebeca (one activation per received message, run to its next blocking point), put wires, adapters and channel properties in an explicit glue layer as BIP does, keep every source of nondeterminism outside the rules in a recorded, replaceable scheduler as Coyote and P do, and carry a path condition in every configuration so that concrete, symbolic and exhaustive runs are three search strategies over the same `enabled`/`fire` pair rather than three engines. DEVS contributes the structural vocabulary and closure under coupling as the formal meaning of "a closed entity is one step-able thing", but its deterministic tie-breaking (`select`, confluent transitions) and real-valued time base are exactly what a race-finding tool must not inherit. Macro steps need three separate relations (activation run-to-completion, summary versus full, collapsed segment), and atomicity has to be computed from read/write/channel footprints, never declared, because wrong grain of atomicity is the best-documented failure of both hand-written and LLM-written models. The co-simulation boundary should always be a port, governed by an FMI-style unit contract with capability flags and a per-port policy borrowed from service virtualization (model, real, spy, capture, diff); the debugger should be a query-first, Pernosco-like tool over a Replay-like store keyed by (model version, binding, schedule, edits); and the compiler should be Specula scaled down to one port per call, with a repair loop capped near three iterations and validation in both directions (traces into the model, model-generated tests onto the code). Most of the concrete design below is inference assembled from adjacent evidence: no surveyed system combines symbolic data inputs, schedule exploration and partial-order traces, none lifts concrete program state into a predicate model automatically, and none has been validated on HTTP, queue and database applications. The first thing to build is therefore a minimal interpreter over the hand-written queue-and-worker gold model, with property tests that the three macro-step relations agree with their small steps.

## What six bodies of precedent settle, and where they stop

### DEVS gives the structure and the meaning of zooming out, not the scheduler or the clock

An atomic DEVS is the tuple ⟨X, Y, S, δint, δext, λ, ta⟩ and a coupled DEVS wires components through influencee sets; the formalism is closed under coupling, meaning any coupled model has an equivalent atomic model whose state is the product of its children's total states and whose time advance is the minimum of their remaining times ([Van Tendeloo & Vangheluwe, arXiv 1701.07697](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07697)). That construction is a citable definition of the checkpoint's "an entity looks the same at every depth": the behavior of a closed entity *is* the resultant of its interior. Two caveats matter. The resultant is an exact product, not an abstraction, so the project's `summary` level is a further claim (a morphism from resultant to summary) that needs its own check; and closure collapses *space* (an entity), not *time* (a segment), so DEVS covers only half of the zoom story. The DEVS note also corrects a premise in the brief: classic coupled DEVS carries a translation function Z_i,j on every connection, including the connections from the outer model to a child and back ([arXiv 1701.07697](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07697)), so "a wire may transform the message" has precedent. Z_i,j is a pure function of one event; the port-based formulation (EIC, IC, EOC) reduced couplings to pure forwarding ([Wikipedia, DEVS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEVS), a secondary source carrying quality tags).

What DEVS does not give is a scheduler suited to race finding. Classic DEVS resolves simultaneous imminent components with a deterministic `select`; Parallel DEVS replaces it with input bags and a confluent transition function whose order the modeler picks ([arXiv 1701.07697](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07697); [OpenModelica DEVSLib guide](https://build.openmodelica.org/Documentation/DESLib.DEVSLib.UsersGuide.ParallelDEVS.html)). Every such place is a choice point the project must enumerate rather than fix. DEVS time is real-valued, with `ta = 0` for transitory states and a documented livelock when zero-time models form a loop ([arXiv 1701.07697](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07697)); two events at the same timestamp are "simultaneous", which is not the same as "incomparable in happens-before". The variants each cover one needed axis: dynamic structure through a network executive that holds structure as state (DSDEVS; [Barros, WSC 1998](https://www.informs-sim.org/wsc98papers/056.PDF)), with PythonPDEVS warning that "after a model is removed, there is no way to get it back" ([PythonPDEVS docs](https://msdl.uantwerpen.be/documentation/PythonPDEVS/dynamicstructure.html)); routing nodes that pair behavior with a routing policy (RDEVS; [SoSyM](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10270-021-00928-4)); and finite, elapsed-time-independent models with finite reachability graphs (FD-DEVS; [IEEE record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/1067656.htm), abstract only). "Symbolic DEVS" symbolizes event *times* as linear polynomials, not data inputs ([record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/864823.htm), abstract only); the note found no DEVS variant with path conditions over named predicates or with a purely logical, partial-order time base. Performance work with the DEVStone benchmark reports that flattening the hierarchy is faster than executing coordinators per level ([arXiv 2309.16544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16544); [WSC 2019](https://www.informs-sim.org/wsc19papers/006.pdf), the latter a search snippet, and no per-engine flattened-versus-hierarchical numbers were retrieved).

### Mixed-abstraction simulation shows how levels coexist, and admits it cannot prove they agree

SystemC TLM-2.0 is the clearest precedent for user-chosen granularity. The same socket carries either a single blocking call with an annotated delay (loosely-timed, two timing points per transaction) or a multi-phase handshake (approximately-timed), and transactors adapt transactions to pin-level RTL ([Embecosm EAN1](https://www.embecosm.com/appnotes/ean1/html/ch02s03s04.html); [Cambridge ESL notes](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/0910/P35/obj2.2/page28.html)). The current standard is IEEE 1666-2023 with reference implementation SystemC 3.0.x ([Accellera](https://accellera.org/news/press-releases/386-accellera-announces-ieee-1666-2023-standard-available-through-ieee-get-program); [release notes](https://github.com/accellera-official/systemc/blob/main/RELEASENOTES.md)). The four base-protocol phases and the generic-payload-plus-ignorable-extensions structure are stated in the notes from training knowledge and were not re-read in the standard. The field's uncomfortable lesson is that agreement between levels is checked, not proven: "equivalence checking between system-level (i.e., TLM) and RTL implementations is still an open problem" ([Bombieri et al., MEMOCODE 2007](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/263822/1/jpms-memocode07.pdf); an old result, with no newer source found claiming it solved), and practice relies on transactor-driven co-simulation, assertion coverage and a base-protocol checker per interface ([UF, VLSI 2013](https://www.cise.ufl.edu/research/cad/Publications/vlsi13assertion.pdf); [Doulos](https://www.doulos.com/knowhow/systemc/tlm-20/)). Temporal decoupling, which lets a thread run ahead inside a quantum, buys speed at the cost of functional errors from missed synchronization ([DATE 2019](https://past.date-conference.com/proceedings-archive/2019/html/0086.html)); that is the hardware name for collapsing a lane that touches shared state.

Virtual platforms show how fidelity changes during a run. gem5 switches CPU models by draining the system, switching out the old CPUs, changing the memory mode and resuming, and restores checkpoints on a simple CPU before switching to a detailed one ([gem5 Simulation.py](https://git.sanchayanmaity.net/sanchayanmaity/gem5/src/commit/82f600e02ded4d7162dd9286f790462b9ab5d554/configs/common/Simulation.py), a historical source; [gem5 20.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.03152)); Simics turns detailed timing on "dynamically when needed" ([Intel](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/simics-simulator-technology.html)). FMI standardizes only the unit: `fmi3DoStep`, early return, state get/set gated by capability flags such as `canGetAndSetFMUState`, and super-dense time for same-instant events, while leaving the orchestrating algorithm to the importer ([FMI 3.0](https://fmi-standard.org/docs/3.0/)). The co-simulation survey places determinism in the orchestrator, via a `select` function or via confluence that is hard to prove for black boxes, and names "expressing the adaptations required to ensure that the coupling is correct" as an open research need ([Gomes et al., arXiv 1702.00686](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1702.00686)). Service virtualization supplies the boundary modes: Hoverfly's capture, simulate, spy ("any requests that can't be matched will be relayed to the remote target"), synthesize, modify and diff ([Hoverfly docs](https://docs.hoverfly.io/en/latest/pages/keyconcepts/modes/modes.html)). ns-3 shows the cost of the two ways to put real code in a model: Direct Code Execution keeps simulated time and determinism but is lightly maintained, while tap-bridge emulation runs in wall-clock time and showed substantial run-to-run differences under load ([DCE status](https://www.nsnam.org/wiki/Direct_Code_Execution_status); [arXiv 2510.27027](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.27027)). No source was found that lifts concrete program state into a symbolic model automatically at a debugger boundary; refinement mappings are written by hand and PObserve's log parsers are hand-written.

### Executable modeling languages converge on small steps, queues and an external resolver

Every language surveyed defines behavior as a transition relation over configurations, and the frameworks that make one definition serve many tools are the direct precedent for "one interpreter, three modes". The K framework's prover "uses the semantics for symbolic execution" ([Runtime Verification](https://runtimeverification.com/blog/k-vs-coq-as-language-verification-frameworks-part-3-of-3)), and a symbolic semantics can be derived mechanically from a concrete one such that feasible symbolic executions and concrete executions mutually simulate each other ([Arusoaie, Lucanu, Rusu](https://hal.umontpellier.fr/CRISTAL-DREAMPAL/hal-01238696v2)). GEMOC plugs any discrete-event operational semantics into an engine whose step notifications feed generic services, including omniscient debugging ([GEMOC](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01355391); [SLE 2015](https://gray.cs.ua.edu/pubs/sle-2015.pdf)). Plotkin's SOS and K's cell-structured configurations are cited in the notes from background knowledge only.

The statechart family is a warning. STATEMATE's semantics was informal, secondary sources count about twenty variants ([Statemate slides](https://chess.eecs.berkeley.edu/design/2010/discussions/slides/Statemate.pdf)), and even the "precise" UML state-machine semantics has open OMG issues on the order of transitions and the atomicity of effect, entry and completion inside one run-to-completion step ([OMG PSSM issues](https://issues.omg.org/issues/spec/PSSM)). Esmaeilsabzali and colleagues deconstruct such languages into orthogonal semantic aspects ([Requirements Engineering 2010](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/%7Ejmatlee/Papers/REJ10c.pdf)); the full list of aspects in the notes is partly from background knowledge, with only some option names confirmed. The actor family is the better fit. In P each machine has an unbounded FIFO buffer, sends are asynchronous, a dequeue takes the first non-deferred event, and an unhandled event is an error the checker reports ([P manual](https://p-org.github.io/P/manual/statemachines/); [PLDI 2013](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/p-safe-asynchronous-event-driven-programming-2/)); Timed Rebeca executes message servers atomically and offers two state spaces for one language, a fine one and a coarse one-transition-per-handler one that is sound only for event-based properties ([arXiv 2309.07302](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.07302)). Erlang guarantees message order only between one sender and one receiver ([erlang-questions](https://erlang.org/pipermail/erlang-questions/2013-January/072133.html)), and a semantics that delivers atomically at send time imposes an order the real system lacks ([Computers 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-431x/13/11/276)). BIP separates behavior, interactions and priorities, with an Engine that computes enabled interactions and picks one ([IEEE Software 2011](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/170496/files/ieee-software.pdf?version=1)); its move from atomic to non-atomic interactions does not preserve observational equivalence without an added oracle ([FORTE 2008](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/978-3-540-68855-6_8)), the cautionary tale for asserting atomicity. Lingua Franca and SCXML remove nondeterminism by logical tags and document order respectively ([LF docs](https://www.lf-lang.org/docs/next/writing-reactors/time-and-timers); [W3C SCXML](https://www.w3.org/TR/scxml/)), which is right for specifying a design and wrong for exposing races in someone else's.

For collapsing steps, the theory is stuttering refinement (Abadi and Lamport) and Lipton reduction; both primary papers are cited in the notes from background knowledge. The verified operational form is CIVL's rule that sequences of right movers followed by left movers are atomic ([CIVL slides](https://fmcad.org/FMCAD21/slides/civl.pdf)), with reduction still being generalized in January 2026 ([arXiv 2601.13341](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13341)). The specific classification "send to an unbounded channel is a left mover, receive is a right mover" was *not* located in a fetched source and must be checked against [Kragl et al., PLDI 2020](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/~cenea/papers/pldi20is.pdf) before it is relied on.

### Exploration tools agree on replayable choices and disagree on how much to enumerate

Explicit-state checkers deduplicate by fingerprint (TLC stores 64-bit hashes and reports collision probability; [TLC options](https://nightly.tlapl.us/doc/model/tlc-options-page.html)), reduce by symmetry over interchangeable identifiers ([Ip & Dill](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00625969)) and by partial order, where DPOR backtracks only at reorderings of dependent operations ([Flanagan & Godefroid](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1040305.1040315)) and optimal DPOR explores exactly one execution per Mazurkiewicz trace ([Abdulla et al., POPL 2014](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2535838.2535845)). Industrial practice nevertheless defaults to sampling: `p check` runs a seeded random strategy and writes a `.schedule` file for replay ([P docs](https://p-org.github.io/P/advanced/debuggingerror)), P's exhaustive checker warns that exhaustive exploration "is generally not feasible for large models" ([PSym](https://p-org.github.io/P/advanced/psym/whatisPSym)), and Fest at NSDI '26 reports 33% more bugs than existing methods by coverage-guided schedule generation on 94 P model configurations, in the authors' own evaluation ([Fest](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi26/presentation/li)). Bounds are cheap and effective: iterative context bounding found many bugs with at most two preemptions ([Musuvathi & Qadeer](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1250734.1250785)), and PCT finds a depth-d bug with probability at least 1/(n·k^(d−1)) per run ([Burckhardt et al.](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1736020.1736040)). Deterministic simulation testing applies the same idea to whole systems, reproducing a failure from seed plus commit ([TigerBeetle VOPR](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/about/vopr)), and Antithesis branches from saved states instead of replaying from the start ([Antithesis](https://antithesis.com/blog/deterministic_hypervisor/)).

Two gaps shape the design. Replay against *changed* code is weak everywhere: a seed reproduces nothing after a change, and no tool documents replay on a modified program beyond "seed plus commit"; DEMi had to be domain-aware to decide which events still correspond after removal ([Scott et al., NSDI 2016](https://cs.nyu.edu/~apanda/assets/papers/nsdi16.pdf)), and Hypothesis keeps reduced cases valid by replaying a recorded choice sequence through the generator ([MacIver & Donaldson](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ECOOP.2020.13)). And no published algorithm diffs concurrent traces across program versions. For web systems the relevant race is database-mediated check-then-act: ACIDRain found 22 vulnerabilities across self-hosted e-commerce applications ([Warszawski & Bailis](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3035918.3064037)), with 17 of 22 attributed to incorrect transaction scoping according to a secondary source ([slides](https://kiarahmani.github.io/files/defense.pdf)); Elle explains anomalies as short named cycles over transactions ([arXiv 2003.10554](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.10554)). No tool was found that detects check-then-act on an abstract model rather than on traces or code.

### Debuggers that work are query-first, and model debuggers already speak DAP

Two storage strategies dominate omniscient debugging: log and index every state change (TOD at about 140 bytes per event; [TOD internals](https://www.pleiad.cl/tod/documentation/internals.html)) or record only nondeterminism and recompute state by deterministic re-execution (rr under 2x slowdown; [arXiv 1705.05937](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.05937); Replay.io computes everything at replay time; [Replay docs](https://docs.replay.io/basics/time-travel/how-does-time-travel-work)). Pernosco combines them and answers gdb from a database, so stepping "just changes an internal timestamp" ([O'Callahan 2019](https://robert.ocallahan.org/2019/11/supercharging-gdb-with-pernosco.html)). The user-facing lesson is that queries beat stepping: Whyline users finished in a median of **4 minutes against 10** for the control group, and novices with it outperformed experts without it ([Ko & Myers, ICSE 2008](https://faculty.washington.edu/ajko/papers/Ko2008JavaWhyline.pdf)); Pernosco's "line executions" evaluate print and condition expressions at every hit without rerunning ([O'Callahan 2019](https://robert.ocallahan.org/2019/11/omniscient-printf-debugging-in-pernosco.html)).

At model level, GEMOC generates a domain-specific, multidimensional trace (one timeline per dynamic field) that uses about 6x less memory than copying the model per step and is at least 2.2x faster at reaching past states, figures taken from an abstract snippet ([JKU summary](https://se.jku.at/omniscient-debugging-for-executable-dsls/)), with simpler semantic differencing ([SoSyM 2019](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/950)). The TLA+ debugger is a working DAP adapter over a model checker, with conditional breakpoints such as `~ENABLED Next`, step-back to predecessors, and, since January 2026, interactive choice of any successor state ([TLA+ debugger docs](https://docs.tlapl.us/_export/raw/using:debugger)). DEVS debuggers add "god events" that alter state mid-simulation ([Debugging Parallel DEVS](https://repository.uantwerpen.be/docman/irua/014f33/144592.pdf)). DAP itself carries `stepBack`, `reverseContinue`, `setVariable`, data breakpoints, `stepInTargets`, `goto` and a stepping `granularity` ([DAP specification](https://microsoft.github.io/debug-adapter-protocol/specification.html)). For conformance there are three families: lockstep monitors over parsed logs (PObserve; [docs](https://p-org.github.io/P/advanced/pobserve/pobserve)), constrained model checking of partial traces, which found discrepancies in every system studied ([Cirstea et al., arXiv 2404.16075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16075v2)), and process-mining alignments, exponential in the worst case and made tractable by windows or prefix-alignments ([arXiv 2406.05439](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05439); [van Zelst et al.](https://pa.win.tue.nl/online-conformance-checking-relating-event-streams-to-process-models-using-prefix-alignments/)). Perfetto's columnar store with an SQL engine is the scale reference ([Perfetto](https://perfetto.dev/docs/design-docs/trace-processor-architecture)). Comprehension evidence exists for time-space diagrams (ShiViz, two studies with 109 students; [TOSEM 2020](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~bestchai/papers/tosem20-shiviz.pdf)) and for trace visualization generally (22% less time, 43% more correct; [Cornelissen et al.](https://azaidman.github.io/publications/cornelissenTSE2010.pdf)); none exists for video-like playback, for flipping schedule choices, or for a debugger that couples spatial zoom with time travel.

### Code-to-model compilation works only with a conformance loop

One-shot or lightly iterated prompting fails on real systems: on SysMoBench's etcd Raft the best 2025-era agent reached **15.38%** conformance ([SysMoBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23130v3)). Specula (July 2026) reaches 100% on the same benchmark's conformance metric with Opus-4.8 by inferring evidence-backed invariants first, writing a reference model, projecting it per scenario, instrumenting the code, replaying traces against the model, repairing either the model or the instrumentation, and then model-checking repaired models against the invariants to catch overfitting; it reports 249 bugs across 48 projects at a median of **$57 and 3.69 hours per project** ([Specula](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25333v1)). All figures are the authors' own, and an independent summary notes the comparison does not isolate which component produces the gain ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.25333)). The error taxonomy is consistent across sources: oversimplified concurrency and wrong grain of atomicity, omitted behaviors together with admitted unreachable states, textbook behavior where the code deviates, and repair that cheats by weakening guards or hard-coding trace updates ([Specula](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25333v1); [SysMoBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23130v3)). LLM-generated state diagrams almost entirely miss self-transitions, which are often timeouts ([arXiv 2511.05165](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.05165v1)), and guards are the weakest element on spec inputs (guard F1 0.23 with older models; [arXiv 2604.00275](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00275)).

Validation practice says the same thing from the other side. Trace validation found grain-of-atomicity mismatches in EWD998 and Raft, with remedies of logging several transitions, composing actions or treating state-preserving steps as stuttering; logging events together with variable values explores the fewest states, while too-sparse logs explode the search ([arXiv 2404.16075](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16075v2)). MongoDB abandoned trace-checking against a highly abstract spec as "more effort than it was worth" while model-based test generation reached 100% branch coverage of the specified algorithm ([arXiv 2006.00915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00915v1)). Repair loops capture most of their gains in the first 3–4 iterations ([arXiv 2607.05197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05197)); local success does not compose (95.7% syntactic against 3.7% verified on multi-function Dafny programs; [DafnyComp](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23061v1)); DEVS-Gen found that forcing a strict formalism on the generator carries a "cognitive penalty" and that structure-first, per-component synthesis with bottom-up interface reconciliation works better ([arXiv 2603.03784](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03784)); and MIRAGE's simulated services jump from 53–75% to 99% body-shape fidelity when the dependency's real source is supplied ([MIRAGE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04806v3)). All conformance evidence comes from consensus protocols, concurrency primitives and three demo microservice systems. No benchmark measures over-approximation automatically, and none covers web applications as executable port-and-behavior models.

## The proposed semantics: a P-style core with explicit glue and a recorded scheduler

Everything in this section is a design inference. Each choice names the precedent it rests on; the combination has not been mechanized or run.

### Configuration

The static model (types, ports, wires, adapters, behaviors, machines, predicates) is the program and is not part of the state. The trace is output and is not part of the state either; two configurations are equal regardless of how they were reached, which is what makes fingerprinting, snapshots, reverse stepping and search well defined.

```
C = ⟨ E, S, L, Ch, A, T, φ, now ⟩

E   : instance-id ⇀ (type, parent per facet, status)     status ∈ starting | running | stopping | dead
S   : state-var-instance ⇀ value | ⊥                     stores; ⊥ = not yet known (symbolic)
L   : lock-or-transaction-id ⇀ holding activation        from pack- or extractor-declared boundaries
Ch  : wire-instance ⇀ sequence of messages               attrs per wire: fifo|bag, lossy, dup, bounded(n)
A   : activation-id ⇀ ⟨instance, behavior, lod, pc, locals, awaiting, cause⟩
T   : set of timers ⟨tag, target activation or port, kind⟩
φ   : path condition: partial valuation of predicate occurrences, plus declared relations
now : superdense tag (t, microstep); orders timers among themselves only

message  m = ⟨id, kind, layers: stack of frames, payload shape, corr?, cause⟩
```

An activation is "the position of a lane": one execution of one port's behavior, created by one received message. Its `pc` is a node of the full graph, or at summary level a pair (set of candidate cases, effect index). Identifiers are minted deterministically from the causing step (`<port>#<n>` for activations, the spawning step's ID plus an index for spawned instances), so replay and diff are stable; this extends the checkpoint's semantic-path rule to runtime identity and has no external precedent.

### Step rules

One step is one rule firing; its label is one event from the checkpoint's fixed vocabulary. The pinned choices that follow from the statechart-variant literature are: exactly one transition per small step (interleaving, with true concurrency recovered in the pomset); no parallel regions inside a behavior (parallelism only through `par` and separate activations, which sidesteps the open PSSM issues); messages persist in channels until received; guards read the current store and path condition at the moment of the step; no priorities in the core, with packs free to declare priorities among their own transitions (the event loop's microtasks before macrotasks, following BIP).

| Rule `[event kind]` | Enabled when | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ENV-IN `[receive]` | the scenario has an input pending on an external port, and its predecessors in the scenario's partial order have fired | append the message to that port's entry wire |
| DELIVER `[route / transform]` | `m` heads `Ch(w)` and `w` ends at a port with no behavior (a delegation hop or boundary) | move `m` to the next wire; a stateless adapter rewrites `m.layers` (push, pop or rewrite a frame); a routing hop picks the target by predicate or by `choose` |
| RECEIVE `[receive]` | `m` heads a wire into provided port `p` of a `running` instance, and the activation bound for `p` is not exceeded | pop `m`; create activation `a` at the behavior entry with `cause = m.id`; bind predicates whose `over` is a field of `m` |
| LOCAL `[action]` | `a.pc` is an action node | advance |
| BRANCH `[assume]` | `a.pc` is a branch with an edge guarded by `g`, and `φ ∧ g ∧ facts(S, locals)` is satisfiable | `φ := φ ∧ g`; follow that edge |
| SEND `[send / queue]` | `a.pc` is a call on required port `r` wired by `w` | append a fresh message to `Ch(w)`; if a reply is expected set `a.awaiting := corr` and optionally add a timeout timer; otherwise advance |
| RESUME `[receive]` | a reply with `corr = a.awaiting` heads its channel | pop; cancel the timer; bind predicates whose `over` is this response; advance |
| TIMEOUT `[error]` | `a` is awaiting and its timer is the earliest in `T` | clear `awaiting`; follow the timeout edge; a later reply is dropped with an orphan marker |
| READ `[read state]` | `a.pc` is a store read of `x` | if `S(x) = ⊥`, split on its value and record the choice in `φ`; copy to locals; bind predicates whose `over` is `x` |
| WRITE / CAS `[write state]` | `a.pc` is a write of `x := v`, or a compare-and-set | `S(x) := v`; legal iff the machine of `x` has a transition `S(x) → v`; otherwise the result is an **illegal-write configuration** |
| ACQUIRE / RELEASE `[wait / hold]` | lock free, or held by `a` | update `L` |
| REPLY `[send]` | `a.pc` is an outcome node | append the reply on the reverse channel; remove `a` if finished |
| PAR / JOIN `[split / merge]` | `par` node; all children finished | create child activations; resume the parent |
| SPAWN / KILL / CRASH `[spawn / die]` | a spawn node in the lifecycle owner's behavior, or an environment fault the run enables | update `E`; wires for the new instance come from templates on its type; messages to a dead instance resolve by the wire's attribute (drop, error reply, reset) |
| CH-FAULT `[error]` | the wire is `lossy` or `dup` and the run enables faults | drop or duplicate the head message |
| SLOT / UNKNOWN | `a.pc` is an unfilled slot or an `unknown` node | no rule: the activation is stuck and reported as a model gap |

Three points refine the operational-semantics note. First, store access is synchronous and atomic at the default level, although stores remain entities with read and write ports in the record model. This reconciles the DEVS discipline of "no shared state outside ports" (which makes every access visible as a port event) with the fact that a row read is one linearization point, not an asynchronous message exchange; expanding the store's port refines one READ into a request, the store's step and a reply. Second, at summary level a case is not chosen at entry. The note's CASE rule conjoins the whole case guard when the activation starts, which would force the interpreter to guess `upload-ok` before the upload and to evaluate `status-is-pending` before the read. Instead the activation holds the set of candidate cases, each predicate is decided by the rule that observes its `over` (RECEIVE for message fields, READ for state variables, RESUME for responses, the binding for configuration), and candidates inconsistent with `φ` are discarded as it goes. This requires summary cases to list their reads, which the earlier report's example omitted. Third, a write the machine forbids is classified, not just flagged: if the value the activation read was overwritten by another lane before the write, it is a race witness; in a single-lane run it is a model-versus-code disagreement.

### What each record family means in one step

| Record family | Role in the step relation |
| --- | --- |
| Entity (type, instance, facets) | an entry of `E`; facets are used only by projection, never by rules |
| Port | the address of a channel end; a provided port with a behavior is where RECEIVE creates activations; a port without one is a DELIVER hop |
| Wire, adapter, channel attributes | an entry of `Ch`; DELIVER and CH-FAULT are its transitions; a stateless adapter is a function on `m.layers`; a stateful one is an interposed entity from a pack |
| Allocation | no step meaning; maps logical instances to physical ones for projection and for fault scope (a host crash kills what is allocated to it) |
| Predicate | an atom of `φ`; its `over` decides which rule binds it; its `scope` (run, per-activation, per-evaluation, state-derived) decides how occurrences are indexed |
| Characteristic and input class | constructors of the initial `φ0` and of ENV-IN messages |
| Store and state variable | an entry of `S`; READ, WRITE and CAS footprints |
| Machine | a constraint on WRITE; it has no activity of its own |
| Behavior (summary cases or full graph) | the program an activation executes |
| Script | the labels of a run, with `after` computed as causal dependence: order within an activation, send before receive, and write before a later dependent access |
| Assumption | a conjunct added to `φ` by a rule firing with an undetermined predicate; or, for "schedule assumptions", a recorded scheduling choice at a point where a dependent alternative was enabled |
| Override, segment | no step meaning; they parameterize projection and stepping |

A configuration is *terminal* when `A`, `Ch` and `T` are empty. Otherwise, when nothing is enabled, it is one of three things that send the user to different fixes: a **model gap** (some activation sits at a slot or `unknown` node, or a message heads a channel no behavior accepts), a **deadlock** (every activation is waiting on a lock or a reply that cannot come), or quiescence waiting for external input. Two further outcomes are not stuck but are reported: the **illegal write** above, and a **zero-time cycle**, a loop of DELIVER or stutter steps with no blocking point, which is the analogue of DEVS's zero-time livelock and is detected statically on the wire graph. The compiler makes every provided port input-enabled by adding an `otherwise` case that ends in an `unknown` node; this satisfies the conformance literature's requirement for input-enabled implementations while keeping P's rule that an unhandled message is an error, because reaching that case is exactly a reported gap.

### Scheduler interface and the four kinds of choice

```
enabled : C → Set<Choice>
fire    : C × Choice → C' × Label             -- pure and total on enabled choices

Choice  = sched(rule, activation-or-wire id, node id)      -- which enabled thing moves
        | data(predicate occurrence, value)                -- which side of an undetermined guard
        | env(input id | timer id | fault(wire, kind, message) | crash(instance))
        | pick(choose-node id, option)                     -- explicit choose, e.g. a replica
```

Every choice is named by semantic ID, never by index into the enabled set. A *schedule* is a list of choices; a *strategy* is a function from the enabled set (and its own state) to a choice. A run is a deterministic function of (model version, binding, schedule, edits). This is the Coyote and P contract, with the addition that IDs must survive a model change.

### Macro steps: three relations, three obligations

The checkpoint's single statement that "a collapsed segment is a macro step that must agree with its small steps" covers three different things.

**Activation run-to-completion.** The maximal sequence of one activation's steps between blocking points (creation, an `await`, a lock wait, the end). It is a scheduling and stepping unit ("step over"), following P, where context switches happen only at creation, send and receive ([P presentation](https://courses.grainger.illinois.edu/cs524/fa2020/p-presentation.pdf)). It is not automatically atomic: its store accesses remain separately visible to other activations.

**Summary versus full.** For every path through the full graph there is exactly one summary case with the same sequence of port-visible labels (sends, store accesses, reply). This is a stuttering simulation whose observation is the port-visible labels; it is checked per behavior by enumerating full-graph paths with loops treated as composites, and it is the same statement as the multi-resolution literature's "false-preserving" principle ([Liu & Verbraeck](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:a131b1f7-fef2-416a-bf26-80953bd57862), search snippet only).

**Collapsed segment and closed entity.** A segment is a contiguous run of one lane's steps, or all steps internal to a closed entity. Its `atomicity` is computed from footprints. Each step has a footprint (variables read, variables written, channels popped, channels pushed, locks taken and released, instances created or killed). The segment is compared against every step of another lane that can be co-enabled with it: in a concrete trace, the steps incomparable with it in the pomset; statically, any behavior that can have a live activation while this one runs, including a second activation of the same port. Classification is tried in order.

| Value | Condition | Theory |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `stutter` | no store effect, no branch, no assumption; only DELIVER through stateless adapters | every step maps to a stutter at the viewed layer (Abadi–Lamport) |
| `isolated` | every step's footprint is disjoint from every co-enabled step of another lane | all steps are both-movers |
| `guarded` | the segment has the shape right movers, at most one non-mover, left movers; in practice it is delimited by a lock, a transaction of sufficient isolation, or a single CAS | Lipton reduction; CIVL's R\*L\* |
| `non-atomic` | none of the above | collapsing would hide an interleaving |

Only the first three may be collapsed automatically. The mover classification of each rule under each channel attribute (the claim that SEND to an unbounded channel is a left mover and RECEIVE a right mover, that a bounded channel makes SEND a non-mover, that READ commutes with READ but not with a co-enabled WRITE) is the part of this design with the weakest sourcing; it needs a written table and a mechanized check before the collapse rule is trusted.

Two notes disagree on how to enforce agreement. The semantics note wants it by construction from footprints; the co-simulation note, citing fifteen years of TLM practice, wants it as a runtime check that runs both levels and compares projections. Both are adopted because they catch different errors. Footprint analysis is sound only if footprints are complete, and a behavior with an `unknown` node or an LLM-omitted effect has an incomplete footprint. So atomicity is computed statically and conservatively (an `unknown` node makes its segment `non-atomic`), and a differential check runs in CI over the gold models and on first expansion of any segment: execute fine, project onto the coarse observation, compare with the coarse run. A mismatch is a model bug at that segment.

A third conflict concerns the evidence for atomicity. The compiler note recommends marking each transition `atomic` with its lock or transaction; the semantics note says atomicity must never be asserted, least of all by the LLM. The resolution is that the compiler records *evidence* (ACQUIRE and RELEASE nodes, transaction boundaries, conditional updates, each anchored and preferably extractor-sourced) and the interpreter computes the conclusion. The `atomic: false` field on machine transitions in the earlier report becomes a derived value.

### Refinement levels, adapters and expansion

There are two refinement axes and one mechanism. A behavior runs at `summary` or `full`; a message crossing a boundary is seen at an outer layer or opened into inner layers. Both are the choice, per behavior or per interposed pack entity, of which program the activation executes, and both keep port identity fixed, as TLM sockets stay fixed when the coding style changes. A stateless one-to-one re-framing (DNAT, a veth hop, deserialization) lives on the wire as an adapter and is a `stutter` step relative to the upper layer; this is DEVS's Z_i,j. Anything with state or with an n-to-m shape (TLS termination, TCP segmentation, HTTP parsing over a stream) is a boundary entity whose behavior comes from a pack, with a summary ("request reaches the service") and a full graph. A routing hop is a DELIVER with a decision, never trivial, as the checkpoint already requires.

Expanding a segment during a run never translates coarse state into fine state at the switch point, which the multi-resolution DEVS literature identifies as the hard, underdetermined step ([MRT-DEVS](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-8954/10/5/174/html), search snippet). Because an activation's creation is level-independent, the engine restores the snapshot at the macro step's start and re-executes at the finer level under the same binding and schedule, the gem5 pattern of "restore, then run detailed". Fine-level state that has no coarse counterpart and predates the segment (a TLS session established earlier) is either reconstructed by re-running from where it was created or introduced as an assumption record, the analogue of gem5's warming error.

### Dynamic structure and time

The entity tree is mostly static; activations and replica instances are the dynamic part. Following DSDEVS, structure is state (`E`) and a named lifecycle owner (orchestrator, supervisor, autoscaler) is the entity whose behavior contains SPAWN and KILL; crashes are environment choices. Because `E` is in the configuration, structural change is journaled like any other state and reverse stepping across it works, avoiding PythonPDEVS's irreversibility. Replicas are instances of one type and are interchangeable for symmetry reduction. Exhaustive runs declare bounds on instances per type and activations per port, and "bound reached" is a reported result.

Time is deliberately minimal. Logical order is the pomset. Timers carry a superdense tag so that timeouts are ordered deterministically among themselves (the Lingua Franca and FMI 3.0 device), while the order of a timeout relative to a message arrival is a scheduler choice, which makes reply-versus-timeout an ordinary race the explorer finds. A timer is created by the SEND it guards, so a symbolic run cannot fire a timeout before its request; predicates the earlier report typed as `over: time` (for example `stale-15m`) become timers or scenario-level environment choices rather than free predicates. Presentation time never enters the rules.

## Three run modes, one schedule format, and a worked race

### Modes are strategies

| Mode | Initial path condition | Strategy over `enabled` | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concrete | total | default deterministic policy: run the activation caused by the most recent event to its next blocking point, ties by semantic ID; or a recorded schedule | one script; steps where more than one scheduling choice was enabled are marked |
| Symbolic | partial | same scheduler; at an undetermined `data` choice take the base choice, emit an assumption, and keep the sibling as a fork stub holding the pre-fork configuration ID | a symbolic execution tree whose edges are assumption records |
| Bounded exhaustive | partial or empty | search over `sched`, `env`, `pick` and optionally `data` choices within declared bounds | schedule classes (one pomset each), races, gaps, deadlocks, illegal writes |
| Sampled | any | seeded random or PCT-style prioritization with depth 1–3 | violating schedules, with no claim of absence |
| Conformance | partial | the observed event stream picks the enabled choice whose label matches; unobserved internal steps are taken eagerly | matched script, or first divergence |

The checkpoint's "exhaustive exploration" is split in two because the evidence says it must be: enumeration within explicit bounds gives a guarantee that is honest only when the bounds are shown ("no violating schedule with at most 2 deliveries of one report, 2 worker replicas, 1 preemption, depth 40"), and beyond small models the industrial default is sampling, which finds bugs and proves nothing. Predicates are named Booleans, so the symbolic "solver" is propositional propagation plus declared relations, with SMT reserved for arithmetic guards. Per-occurrence predicates are indexed by activation (`upload-ok@generate-report#2`), the analogue of fresh symbols per loop iteration; state-derived predicates are never free. The user-facing tree is never state-merged, because merging destroys the per-path script; a backend reachability query that returns one witness binding, replayed concretely, is the right use of merging or bounded model checking later.

On search order the notes differ: the semantics note leans on optimal DPOR, which is naturally stateless and depth-first, and the exploration note on breadth-first search with fingerprints for shortest counterexamples. Configurations here are small canonical values, not process memories, so stateful breadth-first search with fingerprint deduplication, symmetry over replicas and identical requests, and sleep sets is the right first implementation; it yields shortest violating schedules, which are the easiest scripts to watch. Optimal DPOR is the right tool for a different question, "enumerate the distinct scripts", since one Mazurkiewicz trace is one pomset. Independence for either comes from the same footprints used for atomicity, and exploring at the coarsest level whose macro steps are `stutter`, `isolated` or `guarded` is the largest available reduction. Violating schedules are minimized before display, in three passes over the choice list: drop whole inputs and faults (delta debugging, as DEMi does), reduce preemptions ([Jalbert & Sen](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1882291.1882296)), then shrink data choices; every shrunk schedule is valid by construction because it is replayed through `enabled`. Races are reported in Elle's and ACIDRain's vocabulary (lost update, write skew, non-transactional check-then-act) with the dependent pair overlaid on the two lanes.

### Schedule and replay representation

```yaml
run:
  model: reports@9c1e4b7                # model version (content hash of the record set)
  binding: {classes: [...], predicates: {...}}     # φ0
  strategy: recorded                    # default | recorded | bfs | pct(seed, depth)
  schedule:
    - {k: env,   id: "actor:user/port:post-reports#1", cfg: "f:19ab"}
    - {k: sched, act: "reports/api/port:post-reports#1", node: "*", upto: block}
    - {k: env,   fault: {wire: "reports/broker/port:queue/wire", kind: dup, msg: "m:enqueue#1"}}
    - {k: sched, act: "reports/worker[1]/port:task-generate-report#1", node: "n:load"}
    - {k: data,  pred: "reports/worker/pred:upload-ok@generate-report#1", value: true}
  edits: []                             # flips, state assignments, injected messages, each at a step id
```

`upto: block` compresses an uncontested run-to-completion; `cfg` is a fingerprint of the configuration the choice was taken from, used as a check, not as identity. Replay on an unchanged model is exact. Replay on a changed model is greedy and reports one of three verdicts per entry: **matched** (the recorded choice is enabled and is taken), **infeasible** (the choice names a transition that still exists but is not enabled, for example because a lock now blocks it), or **diverged** (the transition no longer exists, or the activation has taken another branch); after a non-match the replay continues under the default policy for that lane and keeps trying later entries. This is DEMi's correspondence problem answered with semantic IDs. No surveyed tool defines replay on changed code, so this policy is the project's own and needs testing.

### The report-generation system in all three modes

The example is the earlier report's system: a FastAPI app exposes `POST /reports`, inserts a `PENDING` row and enqueues a task; two Celery worker replicas consume `generate_report`, load the row, return unless it is `PENDING`, set it to `RUNNING`, page through orders, upload a CSV to object storage, and set `DONE`, with a failed upload resetting to `PENDING` and re-enqueuing up to three retries. The traces below were derived by hand from the rules above; nothing has been executed, and no state counts are claimed.

**Concrete run.** The binding selects the classes `within-limit` and `new-key`, sets `upload-ok` to false for the first worker activation and true for the second, `webhook-configured` to false, and enables no faults. ENV-IN places the request; RECEIVE creates `api/post-reports#1`, which runs to completion: a WRITE inserting the row as `PENDING`, a SEND to the broker wire, a REPLY of 202. After the SEND two things are enabled, the API's REPLY and the broker's DELIVER, so the script marks the point and renders the user's response and the worker's start as incomparable lanes. DELIVER through the broker is a `stutter` segment and collapses. RECEIVE on `worker[0]` creates `generate-report#1`: READ sees `PENDING` and discards the `skip` case; WRITE sets `RUNNING`; the `page-orders` loop is one composite step; SEND to the object store awaits; RESUME binds `upload-ok@#1 = false` and leaves the `retry` and `failed` candidates; the attempt counter decides `attempts-exhausted = false`; WRITE resets `PENDING`; SEND re-enqueues. The second delivery creates `generate-report#2`, which reads `PENDING`, claims, uploads, resumes with `upload-ok@#2 = true`, and writes `DONE`. The configuration is terminal. The read-to-claim segment is `non-atomic` by the static computation, because a second activation of the same port can be co-enabled and writes the variable this one reads, so it is never collapsed even though this run has only one lane at a time.

**Symbolic run.** Leave `upload-ok` and `webhook-configured` unbound. The run proceeds identically until the first RESUME from the object store, where both values of `upload-ok@#1` are satisfiable. The default policy takes the base choice (true), mints the assumption record `a:upload-ok@#1 = true, reason: base-choice, alternatives: [false]`, and stores a fork stub pointing at the pre-RESUME configuration. Clicking the faint branch resumes from that snapshot with the other conjunct; it does not recompute the prefix. Because `attempts-exhausted` is derived from the attempt counter and `max_retries = 3`, the tree over upload outcomes has five leaves (success on attempt one, two, three or four, or `FAILED` after the fourth failure), and each success leaf forks once more on `webhook-configured`, nine paths in all by hand count. Had `upload-ok` been declared run-constant instead of per-activation, the retry path would be unsatisfiable on its second evaluation; had `status-is-pending` been left free instead of state-derived, the tree would contain a `skip` path the store forbids. Both are schema errors the semantics forces into the open.

**Bounded exhaustive run.** Bind `upload-ok` to true and declare bounds: one request, two worker replicas, the broker wire marked `dup` (at-least-once delivery, the Celery pack's `redelivered` alternative) with at most one duplication, preemption bound one. The property is the status machine itself plus "at most one successful upload per report". Within those bounds the explorer distinguishes two outcome classes. In the benign class the two deliveries' accesses do not interleave: one activation reads `PENDING` and writes `RUNNING` before the other reads, and the second takes `skip`. In the violating class, one preemption separates a read from its write:

```yaml
schedule:                                                      # minimized; 1 fault, 1 preemption
  - {k: env,   id: "actor:user/port:post-reports#1"}
  - {k: sched, act: "api/post-reports#1", upto: block}          # insert PENDING, enqueue, reply 202
  - {k: env,   fault: {wire: "broker/queue→worker", kind: dup, msg: "m:enqueue#1"}}
  - {k: sched, rule: RECEIVE, port: "worker[0]/task-generate-report", msg: "m:enqueue#1"}     # → a1
  - {k: sched, rule: RECEIVE, port: "worker[1]/task-generate-report", msg: "m:enqueue#1~dup"} # → a2
  - {k: sched, act: a1, node: "n:load"}      # READ status = PENDING
  - {k: sched, act: a2, node: "n:load"}      # READ status = PENDING   ← the preemption
  - {k: sched, act: a1, node: "n:claim"}     # WRITE PENDING → RUNNING
  - {k: sched, act: a2, node: "n:claim"}     # WRITE RUNNING → RUNNING : illegal write, stale read
  - {k: sched, act: a1, upto: end}           # upload, DONE
  - {k: sched, act: a2, upto: end}           # second upload, DONE → property violated
```

The report names the dependent pair (`a1.n:claim` write and `a2.n:load` read on `store:report/var:status`), labels it a non-transactional check-then-act, classifies the illegal write as a race witness because `a2`'s read was overwritten before its write, and presents the scheduling choice at the seventh entry as a flippable "schedule assumption": had `a2`'s read come after `a1`'s write, `a2` would skip. This is the race the earlier report predicted from a static tier-6 check; the interpreter turns the prediction into a replayable script.

**Fix and replay.** The fix replaces the read-then-write claim with a conditional update (`UPDATE … SET status = 'RUNNING' WHERE id = ? AND status = 'PENDING'`), which the model expresses as a single CAS node at `n:claim` with a branch on whether a row was affected. Replaying the stored schedule against the fixed model matches the first eight entries: both activations still load the row, and `a1`'s CAS succeeds. The ninth entry still matches as a choice (`a2` at `n:claim` is enabled) but the CAS fails, `a2` takes `skip`, and the final entry's remaining steps are reported as diverged: the upload and `DONE` write for `a2` no longer exist on its path. The verdict is "schedule replayed; violation not reproduced; first divergence at `a2.n:claim`", and the trace diff, joined on step IDs, shows one changed step (a write became a failed CAS) and the removed second upload. The read-claim segment is now `guarded`, so it may collapse. A fix by row lock (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`) produces the other verdict: the seventh entry is **infeasible**, because `a2`'s load is blocked by the lock `a1` holds, and replay falls back to the default policy. In both cases "fix verified" requires a second step: a fresh exploration within the same bounds finds no violating schedule, since a fix can move a race instead of removing it.

## Hand-off to real runtimes is a per-port contract with capability flags

The checkpoint's open question, at what depth the interpreter hands off and how results are lifted back, has a structural answer and an unsolved part. The structural answer: the boundary is always a port, never a depth. A behavior whose graph bottoms out (a leaf action the user steps into, an `unknown` node, or a port whose policy says so) is replaced by a *runtime unit* that executes the real code behind that port, while everything the unit calls outward is still a model port. The interpreter is the master and units never advance on their own, which is FMI's division of labor.

```
unit.instantiate(entry: port, inputs: concrete values satisfying φ restricted to this port)
unit.step_until(any outgoing modeled port | limit) → early-return(event, position) | finished(outcome)
unit.respond(event, value)                  -- the model's or the real dependency's answer
unit.snapshot() / restore(s) | replay_to(position)
unit.lift() → {predicate occurrence ↦ bool, state-var writes, sends}        -- abstraction function
capabilities: deterministic, can_snapshot, can_reverse, realtime_only, languages
```

Early return at every outgoing modeled port is FMI's `earlyReturn` and is implemented with function or data breakpoints over DAP, or under rr where it is available; rr's restriction to Linux on supported CPUs and to low-parallelism workloads makes determinism a capability flag, not an assumption, and the notes did not research replay for Node or Python runtimes. Each port carries one of five boundary policies, taken from Hoverfly's modes.

| Policy | Meaning at the port | Provenance of the resulting steps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `model` | the behavior or pack answers | as recorded on the model |
| `real` | the unit runs; outgoing calls go to real dependencies | `observed`, regime wall-clock, not replayable |
| `spy` (default) | the model answers where the binding selects a path free of `unknown` nodes; otherwise hand off | mixed, per step |
| `capture` | run real, record lifted events as evidence and as a conformance trace | `observed` |
| `diff` | run both and compare port-visible labels | a conformance verdict |

Hand-off happens only at a quiescent point for the unit's scope: the activation's creation. If the user asks for real execution in the middle of a macro step, the engine restores the snapshot at the activation's start and re-runs with the unit in place, the same rule as level expansion. Lifted results carry `observed` provenance together with the regime (deterministic replay or wall-clock), the runtime version and the commit; evidence from a non-replayable regime is marked as such because reverse stepping across it re-reads the recording instead of re-executing. An LLM standing in for an unavailable third-party service, in the MIRAGE style, is a sixth, weaker option: its answers are `assumed` and it is not deterministic, so its responses must be recorded as `env` choices to keep the run replayable.

The unsolved part is `lift`. The proposal is that the compiler's model-to-code map makes it mechanical: every predicate has an anchored expression, so the unit evaluates that expression at its anchor (a conditional logpoint) and reports the truth value for the current occurrence; every state write and send has an anchored site. Where the model must commit to something the concrete state does not yet determine, the binding is a prophecy in Abadi and Lamport's sense and is recorded as an assumption that the run later confirms or contradicts. No precedent for this automatic lifting was found, and semantic adaptation at co-simulation boundaries is named in the survey literature as open, so this is the least certain component of the whole design and should be built last.

## The debugger is a query engine over a recomputable trace

**Trace store.** A run's identity is the hash of (model version, binding, schedule, edits); content hashes remain cache keys. Because the recorder is the interpreter itself, record-time overhead is not the constraint and both storage strategies are affordable together. The store holds a step log (step ID, lane, rule, refinement level, parent macro step, `after` edges, the three clocks); per-state-variable change lists, which are GEMOC's dimensions and make watchpoints and "when did this last change" logarithmic lookups; configuration snapshots every k steps and at every macro-step boundary, so any step is reached by a snapshot plus at most k small steps with no divergence risk; and per-wire message-in-flight intervals for the canvas. Fine levels are lazy: a macro step stores its summary and boundary snapshots, and expansion re-runs from the boundary. Tables are columnar (`step`, `var_change`, `message`, `assumption`, `segment`, `choice`) behind an SQL-like engine running in WASM workers beside the renderer, the Perfetto arrangement, which matches the checkpoint's Rust core. The snapshot interval and index sizes need measurement on the gold models; no external number transfers.

**Queries and interaction primitives**, in priority order. An executions panel: select a port, transition, predicate or variable and list every occurrence in the run with chosen expressions evaluated at each, as Pernosco does; a breakpoint is a saved query whose next hit stops playback, and a watchpoint is a query on `var_change`. Why and why-not: "why did this step happen" walks back through the enabling guard, the predicate bindings and the `after` edges; "why didn't this fire" evaluates the guard and names the false conjunct or the assumption that bound it, which is far easier here than in the Java Whyline because every guard is an explicit formula over named predicates. Playback and stepping along refinement levels, where step over is one activation run-to-completion, step into descends one level, and step out runs to the enclosing entity's next port event (the DEVS coordinator's `done`); reverse versions are lookups, not re-execution. A fork picker at every point where more than one choice was enabled, for data choices and scheduling choices alike, as in the TLA+ debugger's interactive exploration. Edits (flip an assumption, assign a state variable, inject a message, pick another scheduling choice) as recorded events that branch the run tree, leaving the parent immutable; the fix-check diff and the what-if diff are then one operation. A diff view that joins runs on step IDs, collapses to entity and port paths with counts before expanding into the differing subtree (Jaeger's method), and falls back to alignment for steps whose behavior node changed. And a lane view with happens-before edges beside the spatial canvas, the form for which comprehension evidence exists.

**DAP mapping.** DAP is a secondary, text-level surface for IDEs and coding agents; camera, zoom, playback speed, diffs and conformance verdicts stay in the project's own protocol because DAP cannot express them.

| DAP concept | Interpreter concept |
| --- | --- |
| thread | lane (activation, or the lane of an entity) |
| stack frames | the refinement path of the current step: scenario, segment, activation, node |
| scopes and variables | the focused entity's state variables, messages in flight at its ports, assumptions in force |
| `next` / `stepIn` / `stepOut` with `granularity` | step over an activation run, descend a level, run to the enclosing entity's next port event |
| `stepInTargets` | the enabled choices at this point |
| `stepBack` / `reverseContinue` | previous step; previous hit of a saved query |
| `goto` | jump to a step ID |
| data breakpoints | watchpoints on state variables |
| conditional breakpoints | predicates over the configuration, including "nothing enabled" |
| `setVariable` / `setExpression` | an edit event that starts a branch; followed by `invalidated` |

**Conformance tiers.** Tier one is lockstep: pack-supplied parsers turn log lines or spans into model events, and the interpreter runs with the observed stream as its scheduler; an event with no matching enabled choice is the divergence, and checking against the pomset instead of a total order tolerates logs that interleave concurrent events arbitrarily. Tier two is constrained search for partial observations, the normal case: explore only runs consistent with the observed events, binding unknown predicates on the way; a found run becomes a script whose matched steps are `observed`, and a predicate the trace forces is promoted from `assumed` to `observed`. Tier three is explanation only: a bounded alignment (windowed, or incremental prefix-alignment for a live stream) that reports log moves and model moves at the first divergence. Unbounded optimal alignment is never run on long traces. A divergence is classified before anything is repaired: model gap, instrumentation or logging error, atomicity mismatch, or code-bug candidate.

## The compiler is Specula at port scale, with two-directional validation

The earlier report's pipeline stands: deterministic extractors build entities, ports, wires, schemas and lists of branch points, effects and write sites; the LLM compiles one provided port per call; checks run cheapest first. The new evidence changes six things.

Compilation order becomes strictly bottom-up within a service, and a callee summary must pass its own checks before any caller consumes it, because summaries passed between units are where local success fails to compose. Each unit is generated in two passes in Specula's order: predicates and invariants with anchors first (what must hold and where the code says so), then cases and transitions that may refer only to those predicates; the invariants become the oracle that repairs are checked against. Every transition is emitted with its model-to-code map (the anchor as instrumentation point, and the abstract variables observable there), which is what makes trace capture and `lift` mechanical instead of a second LLM task. The compiler target remains the constrained record schema, not TLA+ or P: syntax and configuration errors consume the early iterations of formal-language pipelines, and DEVS-Gen measured a penalty for forcing a strict formalism on the generator. An export to P or TLA+ is kept as a differential oracle for the project's own interpreter, not as the engine. The compiler emits evidence for atomicity (lock, transaction and conditional-update nodes) and never a verdict. And every provided port is made input-enabled with an `otherwise` case, with explicit attention to the known blind spots: self-transitions, timeouts and error paths are coverage obligations of their own.

The verification loop for one unit runs in this order: schema, anchors, coverage of extracted branches and effects, guard algebra, an interpreter run of each case (a stuck configuration here is the analogue of SysMoBench's runtime failure), then conformance. Repair is capped at three iterations, after which the unit escalates to a stronger model or to k-sample voting, or is downgraded to `assumed` and surfaced. Every repair is scored as a diff, and one that weakens a guard, adds a catch-all, deletes a case or hard-codes a value is rejected unless coverage and the unit's invariants still hold; the repair's kind is recorded in `checks`.

Validation against the code runs in two directions because each catches what the other cannot. Traces into the model (existing tests and recorded runs, instrumented at the anchors, replayed in conformance mode) detect only over-restriction: a missing transition or a too-strong guard. Model-generated tests onto the code (one per case or input class, with required ports stubbed by the model, comparing port-visible outputs) detect over-approximation and invented behavior, and MongoDB's experience says this direction is the more practical one for abstract models. Refinement levels help the first direction: coarse levels are validated against coarse logs such as HTTP spans and queue events and fine levels against function-level tracing, with `stutter` steps treated as stuttering exactly as the trace-validation literature recommends. Mutation gives an adequacy measure for both: mutate the model (drop a case, negate a guard, merge two steps) and require the conformance suite to kill the mutant; mutate the code at anchored sites and require the model-based tests to notice. The one published threshold is an 85% kill rate, from RTL assertion generation ([arXiv 2606.21451](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21451)), and it is a starting point, not a calibrated target for this domain. Recompilation is triggered when a unit's anchored slices change or when its stored conformance tests fail; a failure without an anchor change indicates an unanchored dependency and is logged as a compiler defect.

## What is evidence, what is inference, and what to do first

**Evidence-backed recommendations**, in the sense that published systems show the pattern working: a small-step relation as the single definition from which debugging, symbolic execution and exploration are derived (K, GEMOC); asynchronous sends into per-channel buffers with run-to-blocking-point handlers and "unhandled is an error" (P, Rebeca); nondeterminism resolved by an external, recorded scheduler with replay (Coyote, P, deterministic simulation testing); bounded exploration with fingerprints, symmetry and partial-order reduction, and sampling beyond small bounds (TLC, P, Fest); execute flat and treat hierarchy as a view (DEVStone); fidelity switching by quiescence and re-execution from a checkpoint (gem5); a unit contract with capability flags and rollback (FMI); checking cross-level agreement by running both levels (TLM practice); query-first debugging over an indexed or recomputable trace (Pernosco, Whyline, GEMOC); a DAP adapter over a model interpreter (TLA+); lockstep and constrained-search conformance, with atomicity mismatch as the dominant source of false divergence; per-unit compilation with a trace-validation loop, an independent oracle against repair overfitting, and a repair cap near three iterations (Specula, SysMoBench, the repair-loop study); and real dependency source over priors (MIRAGE).

**Design inferences**, assembled from adjacent evidence and endorsed as a whole by no source: the configuration tuple and rule set; per-wire channels with attributes; deferred case resolution at summary level; the classification of illegal writes; the four-way footprint computation of atomicity and auto-collapse by reducibility, for which no presentation-oriented precedent was found; semantic-ID schedules and the three replay verdicts on a changed model; schedule assumptions; the pomset diff; the port-level co-simulation contract and automatic lifting; the DAP mapping of stack frames to refinement paths; and model and code mutation as adequacy metrics for a code-to-model compiler.

**Claims whose sources the notes did not verify** and that should be spot-checked before being quoted: Plotkin's SOS, K's cell structure, Redex and DynSem; the Abadi–Lamport and Lipton originals; the BIP composition rule; King's and Flanagan–Godefroid's definitions as stated; the mover status of send and receive; the full list of big-step semantic aspects; TLM-2.0's phases and generic payload; rr's single-core serialization; WireMock's stateful scenarios and Pact; DAP's granularity values; the PDEVS coupled definition (from Wikipedia) and FD-DEVS properties (abstracts and Wikipedia-derived pages); the flattening result in the WSC 2019 paper, MRT-DEVS, ml-DEVS and the false-preserving principle (search snippets); GEMOC's 6x and 2.2x figures, Lewis's and the Moldable Debugger's cost figures, and Perfetto's WASM and WebGL details (snippets or mirrors); the 17-of-22 ACIDRain split (secondary slides); Themis and IsoDiff (search summaries); and, on the compiler side, several snippet-only papers (TLA-Prover, FM-Agent, the drift and self-repair preprints). State-inferer is an unreviewed preprint. The folklore that TLC handles 10^8 to 10^10 states has no citable source and is not used here.

**Risks.** The mover table is unproven for this rule set, and an error there makes the collapse rule hide exactly what it exists to show; database isolation levels complicate it further, since a transaction under READ COMMITTED is not a `guarded` segment, and the reduction literature surveyed does not cover that. Footprints are only as complete as the compiled model, so an omitted effect silently upgrades a segment's atomicity; the differential check mitigates this only on the paths it runs. Interactive latency of bounded exploration is unmeasured; no source gives schedules-per-second for models of this size. Replay on a changed model and pomset diffing have no prior art and may confuse more than they explain when IDs drift. Deferred case resolution requires summaries to list reads in order, raising the bar for the compiler in the area (ordering of effects, grain of atomicity) where LLMs are weakest. Automatic lifting has no precedent. Every conformance result in the literature comes from protocols and concurrency primitives, so its transfer to HTTP, queue and database applications is an assumption; whether OpenTelemetry spans suffice for coarse-level conformance is unverified. And no study supports video-like playback, flipping schedule choices, or zoom-coupled time travel as comprehension aids.

**What to prototype first.** One: write the rules as an executable artifact, either in PLT Redex or as a few hundred lines of Rust with property tests, and hand-write the queue-and-worker gold model in the record schema, including the reads, the `dup` wire attribute and the predicate scopes. Two: run the three modes on it and reproduce this report's worked example; the acceptance test is that the bounded search finds the duplicate-claim schedule unprompted, minimizes it to one fault and one preemption, and that replay against the CAS and row-lock fixes yields the two verdicts described. Three: property-test the macro-step relations (activation runs against small steps; summary against full; every auto-collapsed segment against its expansion under random schedules) and write the mover table while doing so. Four: measure configuration size, snapshot interval, state counts with and without symmetry and reduction, and time to expand a level, against the checkpoint's 100 ms target. Five: export the same model to P or TLA+ and compare reachable outcomes as a differential check on the interpreter. Six: capture one real trace of the scenario and run tier-one conformance. Only then build the compiler's conformance loop for one port, and leave the runtime-unit hand-off for last.

**Proposed changes to checkpoint decisions.** "Scripts are interpreter traces under a binding of inputs" should read "under a binding and a schedule", and reverse stepping should cite run identity as (model version, binding, schedule, edits). "One interpreter, three run modes" should name five strategies over one relation, splitting exhaustive exploration into bounded-exhaustive (with bounds shown as part of the result) and sampled (no claim of absence), and adding conformance. "Granularity is a refinement level; a collapsed segment is a macro step that must agree with its small steps" should be restated as three relations with three obligations, with atomicity always computed. "A stuck configuration is a model gap" should distinguish model gap, deadlock, illegal write (race witness or model–code disagreement) and zero-time cycle. "Messages nest and change form at boundaries" can move from proposed to decided with the rule that a stateless one-to-one transformation is an adapter on a wire and anything stateful is a boundary entity from a pack. "Below the model's depth, hand off to the real runtime" should stay proposed but be reworded: the boundary is a port with a policy (`model`, `real`, `spy`, `capture`, `diff`) and a unit contract with capability flags, and it is built last. The three clocks gain one clarification: timers carry a superdense logical tag, and their order against message arrival is a scheduler choice. The record schema needs four additions and one removal: a `scope` on predicates, channel attributes on wires, ordered reads in summary cases, lock and transaction boundary nodes as anchored evidence, and the `atomic` field on machine transitions becomes derived. The earlier report's pin of SCXML run-to-completion for machines is replaced by P-style activations, with machines reduced to constraints on writes. The open questions "operational semantics" and "DEVS and SystemC TLM" can be closed against this report; the build order gains a step between semantics and gold models, mechanizing the rules and testing macro-step agreement.

## Conclusion

The precedents were expected to supply a semantics to adopt; what they supply instead is a set of separations that every durable tool has converged on. Rules are separate from the resolver of nondeterminism, units are separate from the orchestrator, port identity is separate from the protocol granularity seen at it, the trace is separate from the state, and evidence for atomicity is separate from the verdict. A design that keeps those five separations gets replay, forking, exploration, conformance and level-switching as consequences of one definition, which is why the three run modes in the checkpoint turn out to be strategies and why the debugger's features turn out to be queries.

The same reading shows where the project is alone. Formalisms built for specifying designs remove nondeterminism; tools built for finding races keep it but do not explain or abstract; simulators that mix abstraction levels check agreement empirically and say so. A tool that computes when a collapsed view is faithful, shows a race as a flippable scheduling choice, and replays one schedule across two versions of a model compiled from code has no direct ancestor in any of the six fields. That makes the footprint-based atomicity computation and the semantic-ID schedule the two artifacts most worth getting exactly right, and both can be tested on a hand-written model long before any compiler exists.
